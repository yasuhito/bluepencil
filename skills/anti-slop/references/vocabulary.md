# Words and phrases (rules 1–19)

Adapted from the MIT-licensed [pstack/unslop](https://github.com/cursor/plugins/tree/main/pstack/skills/unslop)
(Lauren Tan), [stop-slop](https://github.com/hardikpandya/stop-slop) (Hardik
Pandya), and [unslop](https://github.com/maxgoff/unslop) (Max Goff).

A word here is a flag, not a ban. If `voice/PROFILE.md` uses it, or the
audience calls the thing by that name, keep it.

## 1. AI vocabulary

Delete or replace with the plain word.

delve, dive into, unpack, shed light on, pave the way, underscore, bolster,
garner, showcase, foster, harness, leverage, elevate, unlock, supercharge,
revolutionize, streamline, empower, enhance, enduring, interplay, intricate,
nuanced, multifaceted, holistic, pivotal, crucial, vital, transformative,
seamless, bespoke, robust, scalable, cutting-edge, best-in-class, world-class,
next-generation, blazing fast, game-changer, tapestry, testament, beacon,
landscape (when it means "situation"), vibrant, synergy.

## 2. Fancy ways to say "is"

"serves as", "stands as", "boasts", "features", "represents", "constitutes".
Write "is" or "has".

## 3. Filler phrases

| Cut or replace | With |
|---|---|
| in order to | to |
| due to the fact that | because |
| it is important to note that | (delete) |
| it is worth noting that | (delete) |
| needless to say | (delete) |
| at the end of the day | (delete) |
| in today's fast-paced world | (delete) |
| when it comes to X | about X, or restructure |
| a wide range of | (name the range) |

## 4. Hedges

arguably, notably, importantly, essentially, fundamentally, inherently,
inevitably, generally speaking, in essence, that said, of course, indeed, to be
clear, quite simply.

Stacked hedges collapse to one word: "could potentially possibly be argued
that it might" becomes "may". State the fact, or say plainly what is unknown.

## 5. Adverbs

really, just, literally, genuinely, honestly, simply, actually, truly, deeply,
very, basically, significantly.

An adverb propping up a weak verb means the verb is wrong. "runs quickly"
becomes "is fast", or the number. "significantly improves" becomes the measured
delta.

## 6. Throat-clearing openers

Any "here's what / here's this / here's why / here's the thing" construction.
Also: "The truth is", "It turns out", "The real X is", "Let me be clear",
"I'm going to be honest", "Can we talk about".

Cut the opener. State the point.

## 7. Emphasis crutches

"Full stop.", "Period.", "Let that sink in.", "Make no mistake", "This matters
because", "Here's why that matters". They add no information.

## 8. Chatbot phrases

"I hope this helps", "Let me know if you have any questions", "Feel free to",
"Happy to help", "Of course!", "Certainly!", "Great question", "I hope this
email finds you well", "As we all know".

Exception: a closing line the profile actually uses. bluepencil's own replies
follow this rule too.

## 9. Meta-commentary

"Let's", "I'll go ahead and", "Now that we have", "With that in place", "As you
can see", "Keep in mind", "Remember that". Do the thing instead of announcing
it.

## 10. Business euphemism

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| navigate (challenges) | handle, deal with |
| unpack (an idea) | explain, examine |
| deep dive | analysis |
| lean into | accept, commit to |
| double down | commit, increase |
| circle back | return to |
| take a step back | reconsider |
| moving forward | next, from now on |
| on the same page | agreed |
| reach out | email, call, ask |
| touch base | talk |
| bandwidth (for time) | time |
| low-hanging fruit | the easy part |

## 11. Abstract metaphor nouns

substrate, wedge, vector, locus, vantage, nexus, primitive (as a noun), surface
(as in "API surface"), bedrock, scaffolding (as metaphor), modality, paradigm,
gold-plating, ratchet (as metaphor), north star, flywheel, endgame.

They read as technical and usually hide a plainer word. substrate → base.
wedge in → add. vector → way, method. gold-plating → more than the job needs.
endgame → the last phase. Pick the concrete word.

## 12. Superficial -ing phrases

"highlighting the importance of", "ensuring a smooth experience", "reflecting
our commitment", "showcasing the results", "fostering collaboration". They
attach to the end of a sentence and add nothing. Delete, or replace with the
fact they were standing in for.

## 13. Vague attributions

"Experts believe", "Industry reports suggest", "Studies show", "Some critics
argue", "It is widely accepted". Name the source, or delete the claim. In
bluepencil's work this is also a facts problem: if the source draft has no
citation, the gap is `[MISSING: source]`.

## 14. Synonym cycling

Calling one thing four names in one passage: the protagonist, the main
character, the central figure, the hero. Pick one name and repeat it. Repetition
of a noun is not a flaw.

## 15. False ranges

"from X to Y" where X and Y are not ends of a real scale: "from onboarding to
enterprise security". List the items instead.

## 16. Generic conclusions

"The future looks bright", "The possibilities are endless", "This is just the
beginning", "Time will tell". State a specific plan, date, or fact, or end the
piece at the previous sentence.

## 17. Lazy extremes

every, always, never, all, none, everyone, nobody, revolutionary, unprecedented
— when they are doing vague work rather than stating a real absolute. Replace
with the actual scope: "every customer" becomes "the 14 customers on the legacy
plan".

## 18. Marketing verbs in commits and release notes

comprehensive, robust, significantly improved, enhanced, various improvements,
major overhaul. Name the actual change.

## 19. Prefer the plain word

utilize → use. facilitate → help. numerous → many. in the event that → if.
commence → start. terminate → end. purchase → buy. attempt → try. The fancier
synonym is rarely clearer.
